# Plan 3 (Hybrid) — Blue vs Red Team Costing Discussion Record

**Facilitator:** Project Manager (PM)  
**Participants:**  
- **Blue Team** — Governance, compliance, process perspective  
- **Red Team** — Operations, execution, vendor perspective  
**Subject:** Review of Plan 3 (Hybrid) Costing Report (RM 123,165)

## 1. OPENING — PM FRAMING

**PM:** *"Both teams have reviewed the costing report. Blue team believes the estimates are too high. Red team believes they are justified. Today's objective is not to win — it's to arrive at a costing that is defensible to senior management, reflects ground reality, and protects the project from budget overrun or underestimation. Let's work through each category methodically."*

---

## 2. BLUE TEAM POSITION — "ESTIMATES ARE TOO HIGH"

### Primary Argument

> *"The total of RM 123,165 is inflated because the plan duplicates resources that already exist in the company, applies corporate-grade governance overhead to what is fundamentally a yard relocation, and stacks contingency on top of contingency. We can deliver this move for RM 85,000–95,000 without compromising safety or compliance."*

### Blue Team — Pros of the Current Estimated Costing

| # | Point | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Comprehensive risk coverage** — security escort, insurance, and contingency are all explicitly budgeted. | No hidden cost surprises; senior management sees full financial picture. |
| P2 | **Governance structure is sound** — 7 gates with sponsor approvals provide accountability. | Each gate is a checkpoint to prevent scope creep and unauthorized spend. |
| P3 | **Documentation and audit trail are thorough** — manifests, custody records, photo evidence. | Protects against disputes with landlords, vendors, and insurers. |
| P4 | **Dedicated roles reduce execution risk** — PM, HSE, Security, Finance are all named. | Clear ownership; no ambiguity about who is responsible for what. |
| P5 | **IMCA compliance is flagged as critical path** — Week 1 priority. | Prevents regulatory catastrophe that could halt the entire operation. |

### Blue Team — Why the Estimates Are Too High

| # | Point | Details |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | **Internal labour (M) is inflated at RM 24,800** | Company already employs yard staff, technicians, and admin. Using existing employees for inventory, packing, and staging costs nothing incremental — they are already on payroll. Budgeting 8 weeks of "additional" labour double-counts. |
| B2 | **PM cost (A1) at RM 9,600 is excessive** | A yard relocation does not require a dedicated PM at RM 1,200/week for 8 weeks. An existing operations lead can manage this part-time. Fair cost: RM 3,000–4,000. |
| B3 | **Contingency is double-counted** | Category N includes 10% contingency (RM 9,065) PLUS separate weather (RM 800), vendor no-show (RM 500), and incident (RM 500) provisions. These should be within the 10%, not additive. |
| B4 | **New yard infrastructure (C) at RM 17,800 should be partially landlord-funded** | Fencing, CCTV, and lighting are leasehold improvements. The Masjid Tanah landlord should contribute or offer a rent-free period in exchange. Budget should only capture the company's share. |
| B5 | **Vendor estimates appear padded** | Heavy haulage at RM 2,500/trip × 3 trips and crane at RM 2,000/day × 2 days are based on generic market rates, not actual quotes. Real quotes may be 15–20% lower. |
| B6 | **Inventory team (B1 + M1) is redundantly budgeted** | Category B budgets 4 persons × 10 days (RM 10,000) and Category M budgets the same team again (RM 10,000). This is a direct duplication. |

### Blue Team — Proposed Adjustments

| # | Proposal | Saving (RM) |
|---|---|---|
| B-A1 | Use existing staff for inventory, packing, staging — remove duplicate M1 line. | 10,000 |
| B-A2 | Reduce PM cost to part-time allocation (RM 4,000 total). | 5,600 |
| B-A3 | Consolidate contingency into single 10% line; remove separate weather/vendor/incident provisions. | 1,800 |
| B-A4 | Negotiate landlord contribution for fencing/CCTV — budget only 50% of C1–C5. | 5,000 |
| B-A5 | Obtain 3 actual vendor quotes before approval; adjust E and G accordingly. | 1,500–2,000 (estimated) |
| | **Total Blue Team Proposed Savings** | **RM 23,900–24,900** |

## 3. RED TEAM POSITION — "ESTIMATES ARE JUSTIFIED"

### Primary Argument

> *"The RM 123,165 figure is not a padded estimate — it is a realistic reflection of what it costs to move an ROV training operation with copper cables, heavy ROV components, and regulatory compliance obligations. Cutting corners on this budget will result in theft, injury, regulatory fines, or project failure. The estimates are, if anything, lean."*

### Red Team — Pros of the Current Estimated Costing

| # | Point | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Estimates are based on actual market rates** — heavy haulage, crane hire, and security escort reflect real vendor pricing from Pasir Gudang–Melaka corridor. | Not theoretical — vendors have been informally approached. |
| P2 | **Security costs are non-negotiable** — copper cables are the highest theft risk (R01 score 20/25, R06 score 16/25). | Without escort and sealed crates, the project risks losing assets worth more than the entire security budget. |
| P3 | **Crane days are based on lift complexity** — ROV components require engineered lift plans, exclusion zones, and competent supervision. | One day is insufficient for multiple heavy lifts; 2 days is the realistic minimum. |
| P4 | **New yard infrastructure is a prerequisite** — Gate 3 cannot be passed without secure cage, CCTV, and utilities. | These are not optional — they are hard requirements before any item arrives. |
| P5 | **IMCA compliance cost is real** — relocating an ROV training establishment has regulatory implications. | Failure to address IMCA could result in training suspension — far costlier than the RM 500 budgeted. |
| P6 | **Vendor communication protocol requires lead time** — 72h booking, 24h confirmation, day-before reminder. | Rushing vendors or using unvetted cheap options increases no-show and damage risk. |

### Red Team — Why the Estimates Are Justified (and where they may be understated)

| # | Point | Details |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Internal labour is NOT duplicated** — existing staff are already at full capacity on daily operations. | Inventory, packing, and staging require dedicated personnel who cannot simultaneously run the yard. Overtime or temporary hires are genuinely incremental. |
| R2 | **Weather delays are understated** — monsoon season (Oct–Dec) is the planned move window. | Heavy lifts cannot proceed in high wind or rain. One weather delay can cascade 3–5 days. RM 800 is insufficient; RM 2,000–3,000 is realistic. |
| R3 | **Packing materials for ROV electronics are underestimated** — anti-static packaging, custom crates, and shock indicators are specialized. | RM 400 (D2) is too low for protecting sensitive ROV electronics. Realistic: RM 800–1,000. |
| R4 | **IMCA compliance may cost more than RM 500** — re-approval, documentation, and potential site audit. | Regulatory consultations and IMCA documentation updates could reach RM 2,000–3,000. |
| R5 | **Old yard security during transition is understated** — copper theft risk persists until the yard is fully cleared. | 5 days of increased security (H2) is likely insufficient; 10–14 days is more realistic. |
| R6 | **Vehicle breakdown / no-show buffer is insufficient** — RM 500 for backup vendor activation is too low. | Emergency vendor call-out typically costs 30–50% above contracted rates. RM 1,500 is more realistic. |

### Red Team — Proposed Adjustments

| # | Proposal | Additional Cost (RM) |
|---|---|---|
| R-A1 | Increase weather delay provision to RM 2,500. | +1,700 |
| R-A2 | Increase anti-static/electronic packaging to RM 900. | +500 |
| R-A3 | Increase IMCA compliance budget to RM 2,000. | +1,500 |
| R-A4 | Extend old yard security to 10 days. | +1,000 |
| R-A5 | Increase vendor no-show buffer to RM 1,500. | +1,000 |
| | **Total Red Team Proposed Additions** | **RM 5,700** |

## 5. FACILITATED RESOLUTION — AGREED ADJUSTMENTS

The PM facilitated the following resolutions, with both teams conceding on key points:

### Resolution 1 — Internal Labour (D1, D8)

**Blue concedes:** Existing staff cannot absorb inventory, packing, and staging while maintaining daily operations. Dedicated personnel are needed.

**Red concedes:** Category B1 and M1 are duplicative. The inventory team should be budgeted once.

**Agreed:** Budget inventory team once under Category B (RM 10,000). Remove duplicate M1 line. Remaining internal labour (M2–M7) stays at RM 14,800.

**Net adjustment:** **–RM 10,000**

---

### Resolution 2 — Contingency Structure (D2)

**Blue concedes:** Separate weather and vendor no-show provisions are real risks, not padding.

**Red concedes:** The incident response fund (RM 500) can be absorbed into the 10% contingency.

**Agreed:** Single 10% contingency (RM 9,065) + weather provision (RM 800) + vendor no-show buffer (RM 500). Remove O2 incident response fund.

**Net adjustment:** **–RM 500**

---

### Resolution 3 — New Yard Infrastructure (D3)

**Blue concedes:** The Masjid Tanah landlord has not committed to contributing; assuming 50% funding is unrealistic.

**Red concedes:** The company should still attempt to negotiate, but budget must assume full cost.

**Agreed:** Budget full infrastructure cost (RM 17,800). PM to attempt landlord negotiation separately; any contribution will be a credit, not a budget reduction.

**Net adjustment:** **RM 0** (no change to budget)

---

### Resolution 4 — PM Cost (D4)

**Blue concedes:** An 8-week relocation with 7 gates and multiple stakeholders does require dedicated PM attention.

**Red concedes:** RM 1,200/week is at the upper bound; RM 900–1,000/week is more appropriate for this scope.

**Agreed:** Reduce PM cost to RM 1,000/week (RM 8,000 total).

**Net adjustment:** **–RM 1,600**

---

### Resolution 5 — Weather Delay Provision (D5)

**Blue concedes:** Monsoon season is a real risk; 8 weeks Oct–Dec will face weather disruptions.

**Red concedes:** RM 2,500 may be excessive; RM 1,500 is a reasonable middle ground.

**Agreed:** Set weather delay provision at RM 1,500.

**Net adjustment:** **+RM 700**

---

### Resolution 6 — IMCA Compliance (D6)

**Blue concedes:** IMCA re-approval is a regulatory requirement, not optional.

**Red concedes:** RM 2,000 is a worst-case scenario; RM 1,000 is a prudent budget.

**Agreed:** Set IMCA compliance budget at RM 1,000.

**Net adjustment:** **+RM 500**

---

### Resolution 7 — Vendor Estimates (D7)

**Blue concedes:** Informal vendor approaches provide reasonable confidence in the estimates.

**Red concedes:** 3 formal quotes may reveal 10–15% savings on some items.

**Agreed:** Budget at current estimates. PM to obtain 3 quotes per vendor before contract award. Any savings to be credited back.

**Net adjustment:** **RM 0** (no change to budget; savings to be realized)
