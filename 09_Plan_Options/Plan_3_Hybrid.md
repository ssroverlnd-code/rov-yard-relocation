# Plan 3 — Hybrid: Governance + Execution (Recommended)

**Approach:** Blue Team's formal governance, compliance, and gate structure + Red Team's operational detail, vendor schedule, and communication plan.  
**Duration:** 8 weeks (with acceleration to 6–7 weeks achievable)  
**Status:** For team discussion and decision

---

## 1. Philosophy

**Formal governance with operational muscle.** Every gate is approved by the sponsor (Blue), but every stage has a printable checklist that foremen use on the ground (Red). The company gets compliance assurance *and* execution speed.

## 3. Inventory Classification (Blue's 6 Categories + Red's Tagging)

| Class | Examples | Package Tag Prefix | Handling |
|-------|----------|--------------------|----------|
| **A — High Value** | ROV electronics, sensors, copper, specialised tools | [ZONE]-A-PKG-[SEQ] | Sealed crates, security escort, secure cage |
| **B — Heavy / Oversized** | ROV frames, machinery, skids | [ZONE]-B-PKG-[SEQ] | Engineered lift plan, low-loader, crane |
| **C — General Equipment** | Racks, workbenches, workshop items | [ZONE]-C-PKG-[SEQ] | Standard transport |
| **D — Loose Items** | Parts, fittings, fasteners, consumables | [ZONE]-D-PKG-[SEQ] | Count, label, bin and box |
| **E — Documents / IT** | Filing cabinets, computers, records | [ZONE]-E-PKG-[SEQ] | Controlled custody, data backup |
| **F — Disposal / Scrap** | Furniture, obsolete, damaged | [ZONE]-F-PKG-[SEQ] | Technical owner confirms, authorized sale/dispose |

**The tagging unit is the package** (sealed crate, bin, pallet, wrapped bundle or container), applied after the sort — 5S: **Sort → Set in order**. Item-level detail goes on the package's contents list and in the inventory spreadsheet, not on a physical tag.

**Condition codes:** SVC / REP / UNS / DAM / UNK  
**Rule:** Do not dispose solely on appearance — technical owner must confirm.

| Week | Focus | Key Activities |
|------|-------|----------------|

| **W6** | Heavy finish + Loose items | **Gate 6 passed** → complete major equipment, consumables transport, multiple vehicle runs |
| **W7** | Loose items complete + Reconciliation | Final packing/transport, shelving, **inventory reconciliation**, furniture disposal deadline |
| **W8** | Close-out + Handover | Full audit, old yard clean-up, **Gate 7 + Gate 8** → handover, closure report |

## 5. Stage Detail (7 Gates — Blue Structure + Red Checklists)

### Stage 1 — Mobilization & Immediate Security (W1)

**Blue:** Appoint PM and functional leads, kickoff and walkthrough, secure vulnerable storage, control all asset movements, establish stocktake template, confirm new-yard representative.

**Red adds:** Set up project folder, WhatsApp group, email thread, photo/video walkthrough, register authority contacts and vendor shortlist.

**First 5 working days (Blue):**

| Priority | Action | Owner | Deadline |
|----------|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Appoint PM and working committee | Management | Day 1 |
| 2 | Secure vulnerable inventory | Yard Supervisor | Day 1–2 |
| 3 | Kickoff and inventory zoning | PM | Day 2 |
| 4 | Begin stocktake and photos | Inventory | Day 2 |
| 5 | Joint new-yard inspection | PM / Yard Owner | Day 3 |
| 6 | Survey heavy loads and vendors | Technical / Logistics | Day 3–4 |
| 7 | Compliance and tenancy review | Admin / HSE | Day 4 |
| 8 | Present preliminary budget, schedule, risks | PM | Day 5 |

**🔒 Gate 1:** Team appointed and temporary security implemented.

### Stage 2 — Inventory & Classification (W1–W2)

**Blue:** Section-by-section stocktake, 6 categories, condition codes, discrepancy register, technical confirmation for disposals.

**Red adds:** Package tagging with `[ZONE]-[CLASS]-PKG-[SEQ]` and a contents list on every package, photo documentation for Class A packages, inventory spreadsheet with full columns, variance report against last known records. Tagging follows the sort — 5S: **Sort → Set in order**.

**🔒 Gate 2:** Inventory baseline and disposal candidates approved by Yard Supervisor, Finance, PM.

### Stage 3 — New-Yard Coordination, Layout & Readiness (W2–W4)

**Blue:** Confirm lease, joint condition survey, measure gates/roads/load capacities, prepare layout, check security, confirm utilities, mark zones.

**Red adds:** 5-zone layout (Secure Cage / Heavy Hard Standing / Shelving / Document Storage / Loading), colour-coded floor markings, fencing/lighting upgrade, CCTV/alarm, fire extinguishers, vehicle one-way loop.

**🔒 Gate 3:** PM, Yard Owner, and HSE approve receiving-site readiness before dispatch.

### Stage 4 — Vendor Procurement, Disposal & Packing (W2–W4)

**Blue:** Transport survey, vendor appointment (capability/safety/insurance/cost), lift plans, packing procurement, furniture sale/disposal, scrap verification.

**Red adds:** 6 named vendor types (heavy haulage, movers, crane, security escort, scrap collector, e-waste recycler), 3 quotes each, vendor communication protocol (72h booking, 24h confirmation, day-before reminder), packing materials per item class.

**🔒 Gate 4:** Vendor orders, disposal approvals, packing lists, shipment schedule ready.

### Stage 5 — High-Value & Sensitive Assets (W4–W5)

**Blue:** Segregate valuables, verify IDs/serial numbers, photograph condition, protect electronics, signed manifest, two-person verification, item-by-item receipt.

**Red adds:** Tamper-evident sealed crates, security escort convoy, direct route (no stops), 30-min checkpoint calls, dual-signature at loading, offload directly into secure cage, reconcile manifest at receipt.

**🔒 Gate 5:** All high-value assets accounted for, received, secured, or exceptions escalated.

### Stage 6 — Heavy & Oversized Equipment (W5–W6)

**Blue:** Confirm weight/dimensions/lifting points/centre of gravity, dismantling approval, lifting plan + risk assessment, crane/forklift capacity, exclusion zones, competent supervision.

**Red adds:** Crane booking per lift day, JPJ oversized load permit if needed, police/authority notification, pre-marked placement zones at new yard, safety briefing every lift day, document damage/missing components.

**🔒 Gate 6:** Major equipment delivered, inspected, safely positioned.

### Stage 7 — Loose Items, Office Equipment & Records (W6–W7)

**Blue:** Hand tools, fasteners, consumables, cables, filing cabinets, IT backup, quarantine unidentified items, sequential labels.

**Red adds:** Packing protocols (bins with dividers, tool rolls, anti-static wrap, secondary containment for chemicals), multiple vehicle runs, shelving by category at new yard, update inventory with location references.

**🔒 Gate 7:** All retained items transferred and disposals documented.

### Stage 8 — Reconciliation, Clearance & Handover (W7–W8)

**Blue:** Match dispatch/receipt, inspect engineering assets, verify bins/storage, test security/utilities, resolve claims, clear old yard, close utilities, closure report.

**Red adds:** Full physical audit (100% match required), commission new yard infrastructure, old yard final clean-up, handover to landlord with photos, close authority licences, lessons learned meeting, sign-off sheet.

**🔒 Gate 8:** New yard accepted, old yard handed over, project closed.

## 10. Acceleration Strategy

Six to seven weeks may be achievable if:
- Teams inventory separate areas in parallel ( warehouse, roofed yard, open yard simultaneously).
- Site preparation and procurement overlap with inventory (W2 onwards).
- Approved scrap/furniture clears early (W3).
- High-value assets move once secure receiving storage is ready (W4, after Gate 3).
- Heavy loads are consolidated (single crane day if possible).
- Reconciliation occurs after each delivery, not only at the end.

**Do not bypass safety, compliance, or site-readiness gates.**

## 14. Why Hybrid Is Recommended

1. **Compliance is not optional.** IMCA, DOSH, DOE, JPJ screening is legally and contractually necessary for an ROV training operation with heavy lifts. Plan 2 would expose the company to regulatory risk.

2. **HSE is non-negotiable.** Heavy lifts, workplace safety, and lifting machinery require a dedicated HSE officer for DOSH/JKKP compliance. Folding this into a Rigging Supervisor is insufficient.

3. **Execution teams need actionable detail.** Plan 1 describes what should happen; Plan 2 describes exactly how. Workers need checklists, packing protocols, and communication templates.

4. **Resource optimization means right-sizing process.** Gates are proportionate — strict for valuables and heavy items, lighter for consumables. This avoids over-process on low-risk moves while protecting high-risk moves.

5. **The 6-category inventory is more accurate.** Documents/IT need custody chains and data backup; merging them with office furniture (Plan 2's Class D) is too coarse.

6. **Quantified risks enable better decisions.** When resources are limited, scoring tells you where to spend first. Plan 1's "High/Medium" doesn't prioritise within the same tier.

7. **The acceleration strategy is real.** Plan 1's 6–7 week target is achievable — but only with Plan 2's vendor schedule, dependency map, and communication protocol to compress the critical path.
